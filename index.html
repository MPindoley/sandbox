One plan, six disciplines

Libbey cut these glasses in Toledo in 1904, every piece from one pattern. A financial plan should hold together the same way: each decision cut to fit the others.

  • Investment management

    Evidence-based portfolios, rebalanced and tax-loss harvested, kept in line with your plan when markets make discipline hardest.

  • Financial planning

    Income, spending, assets, debts, goals and timeline mapped into one living roadmap, so your savings, taxes, insurance and investments point the same way.

  • Wealth accumulation

    The right accounts, contributions and allocation for your risk and time horizon, so what you earn compounds on purpose.

  • Retirement income

    Social Security timing, withdrawal order, Roth conversion windows and layered income for a retirement that may last thirty years.

  • Estate and legacy

    Beneficiaries, trusts, powers of attorney, healthcare directives and giving, coordinated so your wealth reaches the people you choose.

  • Insurance and tax

    Life, disability, long-term care and liability cover reviewed alongside bracket management, so one event or one tax bill can’t undo years of work.

  • Where it was made

    The box shop of the Ford plate glass plant at Rossford, across the Maumee from Toledo, in 1904.

Built slowly, in four phases

Glass that cools too fast cracks, so it is cooled for hours before it is finished. A plan rushed into place fails the same way. Every engagement moves through four phases, at your pace.

  1. Discovery

    We start by listening: your goals, your values, your whole financial picture, and what wealth means to you.

  2. Analysis

    Every account, policy and document is gathered and measured, so we can see where you stand, the gaps and the risks.

  3. Strategy

    One written plan that connects investments, taxes, insurance, retirement income and estate, in the order that matters.

  4. Partnership

    Then the long part: regular reviews, rebalancing and changes as your life, the markets and the law move.

Fees, asked and answered

Three structures, by size. Under $1M, breakpoints apply: cross $250K and your entire portfolio moves to 0.80%; cross $500K and it moves to 0.70%. From $1M to $30M, rates work like tax brackets: the first $5M at 0.50%, the next portion to $15M at 0.25%, and the next to $30M at 0.175%. Above $30M, one flat 0.25% applies to everything.

No. If you hold only a money market, a single bond or a single CD, with no broader managed portfolio, there is no advisory fee. If those positions sit inside a managed portfolio, they are included in its fee along with quieter, low-activity holdings.

Only on certain long-held product structures where a commission is built into the product, and only when that product is the right solution for you. It is never the reason for a recommendation, and it is always disclosed before you decide. Money markets carry no commission of any kind.

No. Expense ratios are built into the funds themselves and are separate from my fee. Keeping them low is part of how I build every portfolio, because each basis point of fund cost is a drag on your return.

At a regulated third-party custodian, never with me. I hold discretionary management authority, but your assets remain in your name at a regulated institution at all times.
